The tested scaffolds for persuasive copy: AIDA, PAS, BAB, 4Ps, PASTOR,
QUEST. Pick the one the goal actually needs, then keep the promise.

## What it does

`SKILL.md` in this directory names six formulas, states what each one is
for, and enforces the structure that makes them work. It is not a swipe
file; it is the rules that make swipe files land.

## When to use this

Load when writing a landing page, a launch announcement, a paid ad, a
cold email, or a product page where the reader taking an action is the
job. Do not load for reference docs, long-form journalism, or internal
comms where the reader has to read it anyway.

## Quick start

You are writing a landing page for a support-triage tool aimed at a
technical audience. The pain is known (Sunday pages), the ask is
moderate (book a call). PAS is right; AIDA is overkill because the
audience is not cold.

```
Problem
   The oncall rotation is stealing Sunday.
   You know because you cancelled brunch again last week.

Agitate
   The average team on our platform used to lose 7.3 hours a
   weekend to pages that did not need a human. That is 30
   weekends a year.

Solve
   Triage takes those 7.3 hours and turns them into 42 minutes,
   because the pages that do not need a human never reach you.

CTA
   Reply with the size of your oncall rotation. We will send back
   the median hours we saved a team that size last quarter.
```

Note what this does not do: no adjectives, no "real-time", no "our
platform", one CTA with a measured ask.

## Key concepts

**Pick from the goal, not the format.** A landing page is not a
formula; it is a container. What matters is whether the reader is cold
(AIDA), in pain (PAS), or already primed (BAB / 4Ps).

**The promise test.** Read the first paragraph and ask what you just
promised. Read the last one and ask if you kept it. Mismatches are
bait-and-switch, and the reader feels it.

**Specificity beats sophistication.** Numbers over adjectives. A named
week over "regularly". A quoted line over a paraphrase.

**One CTA per page.** Two is a coin flip, three is a shrug.

**Read it out loud.** Sentences that trip your tongue trip the eye.
Contractions are usually right.

## Common pitfalls

Bad: opening with an industry stat or `in today's fast-paced world`.

Good: opening with a line from the reader's own week.

Bad: writing features. "Real-time dashboards. Custom alerts. SSO."

Good: writing outcomes. "Your ops lead stops checking the pager on
Sundays."

Bad: hedging the CTA. "Feel free to reach out."

Good: a measured verb. "Reply with the size of your rotation."

Bad: adjectives stacked. `A revolutionary, game-changing solution.`

Good: numbers. "Ten thousand teams use this to run stand-up."
