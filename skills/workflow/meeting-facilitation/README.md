Meetings are a purchase decision. Facilitation is the craft of making
sure the buyer gets what they paid for.

## What it does

`SKILL.md` in this directory defines a repeatable pass for running a
meeting: agenda by decision type, time budgets that hold, an explicit
decision mode (RAPID / DACI) so the room knows who decides, actions
captured with an owner and a date. It is not a template pack; it is the
rules that make templates work.

## When to use this

Load before designing an agenda, running a decision meeting, or
diagnosing why a recurring meeting is not producing outcomes. Do not load
for one-on-ones, interviews, or working sessions where discovery is the
point.

## Quick start

An agenda that survives contact with the room:

```
9:00  Decide: pick two of five Q3 initiatives (15 min)
        Mode: RAPID -- Recommend PM, Decide Head of Product
        Contributors: Design, Data, Eng
9:15  Align: FY26 hiring plan revision (10 min)
        Owner: HRBP; pre-read circulated
9:25  Explore: how do we handle the surge in support tickets? (10 min)
        Trigger question: what is the smallest thing we could test in
        the next two weeks?
9:35  Actions read-out (5 min)
9:40  End
```

The first two minutes: state the decision the meeting exists to make,
out loud. If the decider says "we do not actually need to decide today",
cancel and reschedule.

At 80% of each item's budget, announce the transition. If the item cannot
land, ask once: push the deadline, drop the item, or make a smaller
decision now. Honour the answer.

## Key concepts

**Every item is decide, align, or explore.** Anything else is a status
report. Kill it or convert it.

**Consensus is expensive.** Name the decider using RAPID or DACI at the
top of each decide item, or the meeting after the meeting will be the
real decision.

**Silence is data.** A long silence after a proposal is usually
reservation, not agreement. Name it explicitly rather than assume
alignment.

**Actions need an owner and a date.** Without both, they are wishes.
Capture them in the tracker, not the chat, and read them aloud at the
end so owners hear their name.

## Common pitfalls

Bad: an agenda item titled "Q3 planning".

Good: "Decide: which two of five initiatives move into Q3 (owner: PM,
15 min)."

Bad: extending the meeting because a decide item ran long.

Good: pausing at the 80% mark and asking push, drop, or smaller.

Bad: closing with "great discussion, let's follow up."

Good: reading decisions and actions with owners and dates out loud.

Bad: a weekly sync that never gets reviewed.

Good: a review date on the invite; kill meetings that cannot re-earn
their slot.
